Okay, pivoting from Backpack Spot to Backpack **Perpetuals** (Futures). Let's assess the damage and required adjustments. This isn't just swapping one endpoint for another; it fundamentally changes the strategy leg and introduces new complexities.

**Impact Assessment: HL Perp vs. BP Perp**

1.  **Strategy Core:** The fundamental idea (arbitrage funding rate differences) remains the same, but now it's Perp vs. Perp instead of Perp vs. Spot.
2.  **Backpack API:** You *already confirmed* endpoints exist for Backpack perpetuals:
    *   Funding Rate: `/api/v1/markPrices` (provides current rate) and `/api/v1/fundingRates` (historical). **This is viable.**
    *   Positions: `/api/v1/position`. **This is viable.**
    *   Trading: Standard order endpoints likely apply to perps. **Viable.**
    *   This is *better* than the spot fallback, as both legs are now derivative contracts subject to funding.

3.  **Data Handling:** Needs to fetch and handle Backpack *perpetual* market data (order books, tickers, trades) in addition to Hyperliquid's. Symbols will change (e.g., `BTC_USDC_PERP` instead of `BTC_USDC`). Needs configuration updates.
4.  **Signal Generation:**
    *   NFD Calculation: Becomes `NFD = FR[HL] - FR[BP]`. More direct comparison.
    *   Basis Volatility: Now you need `Basis = MarkPrice[HL] - MarkPrice[BP]`. You need reliable, synchronized mark prices from both exchanges. Basis risk still exists due to potential mark price divergence.
    *   Costs: Need to account for trading fees and estimated slippage on *both* perpetual legs.

5.  **Risk Management:**
    *   **Leverage:** Both legs are now potentially leveraged. Your total exposure calculation and leverage limits must account for this. You cannot simply add USD position sizes; you need to consider the notional value and margin requirements of *both* perp positions.
    *   **Liquidation Risk:** Now exists on *both* legs. Risk Manager must monitor margin usage and distance-to-liquidation on both Hyperliquid and Backpack positions. This is significantly more complex than managing risk for one perp and one spot leg.
    *   **Cross-Margin:** Does Backpack offer cross-margin? Does Hyperliquid? Understanding margin modes is critical. Isolated margin is simpler but less capital efficient. Cross-margin requires careful monitoring of total portfolio margin health.
    *   **Sizing:** Position sizing needs to consider the combined margin impact and risk of two leveraged positions. Simple fixed fraction might still work initially, but the *interpretation* of exposure changes.

6.  **Execution Handler:**
    *   Still needs to execute two legs.
    *   Failure compensation (reversing leg 1 if leg 2 fails) becomes potentially more complex and risky due to leverage and potential for faster price moves on both sides.

7.  **Portfolio Tracker:** Needs to track positions, margin usage, leverage, and P&L for perpetuals on *both* exchanges.
8.  **Balance Monitor:** Still relevant for ensuring sufficient *margin* collateral on both exchanges, not just spot balance.

**Advantages of Perp vs. Perp:**

*   **Purer Funding Play:** Directly captures the funding rate differential between two similar instruments.
*   **Potentially Higher Returns:** Leverage on both legs can amplify returns (and losses).
*   **API Data Availability:** Based on your findings, the necessary data (funding rate, positions) seems directly available via Backpack API, removing the major uncertainty of the previous Spot plan.

**Disadvantages/New Complexities:**

*   **Increased Risk:** Leverage on both legs significantly increases potential losses and liquidation risk.
*   **Margin Management:** Requires managing margin on two exchanges simultaneously. Insufficient margin on one can cascade.
*   **Basis Risk:** Still exists between the mark prices of the two exchanges' contracts.
*   **Liquidation Risk:** Doubled. Need robust monitoring for both positions.
*   **Execution Complexity:** Compensating failures on two leveraged legs is riskier.

**Decision & Mandate:**

Switching to HL Perp vs. BP Perp is **feasible** given the confirmed Backpack API endpoints, and arguably a "purer" implementation of the funding rate strategy. However, it **significantly increases the risk management complexity** compared to the Perp vs. Spot approach.

    *   Implement **liquidation price monitoring** for both positions.
    *   Risk Manager *must* check available margin and estimated margin impact *before* approving trades.

    *   Start with **very low leverage** (e.g., 1-2x max effective leverage) for the prototype.
